Getting started with games

Games are one of the most popular forms of entertainment in the world, and they come in many shapes: mobile games you play for five minutes, console and PC games with long stories, and tabletop games played around a table. Starting well means choosing something that fits your time, budget and taste.

Check the age rating

Most regions use rating systems such as PEGI or ESRB. The label tells you the suggested minimum age and why, for example violence or online interaction. Reading it takes ten seconds and avoids unpleasant surprises, especially when buying for children.

Beginner tip: try demos, free trials or a friend's copy before buying. Play the tutorial fully, because it teaches controls that the rest of the game assumes you know.

Video game genres explained

A genre describes how a game plays, not how it looks. Knowing the main ones makes it much easier to find games you will enjoy.

Action and adventure

Fast movement, combat and exploration. These games reward quick reactions and learning enemy patterns.

Puzzle

Logic, pattern matching and planning. Many are short, calm and suitable for all ages.

Strategy

You manage resources, units or cities and plan several moves ahead. Patience matters more than speed.

Role-playing (RPG)

You build a character, follow a story and make choices. Sessions can be long, so check how much time you have.

Sports and racing

Football, racing and similar simulations. Easy to pick up and great for playing with friends.

Simulation and building

Run a farm, build a town or design a park. Creative, relaxed and open-ended.

How to choose

  1. Think of a movie, book or hobby you enjoy, and look for a genre with the same mood.
  2. Decide how long a session should be: 5 minutes, 30 minutes or an evening.
  3. Read several independent reviews, not only the store description.
  4. Check whether the game is single-player, local multiplayer or online.

Board games and card games

Tabletop games are a simple way to spend time together without screens. They teach turn-taking, planning and good sportsmanship.

Classic games for beginners

  • Chess: two players, 16 pieces each, and the aim is to checkmate the opposing king. It builds planning and concentration.
  • Ludo: a family race game where players move four tokens around the board based on dice rolls.
  • Snakes and ladders: a luck-based game that is ideal for young children.
  • Uno and similar card games: match colours or numbers and be first to empty your hand.
  • Scrabble and word games: improve vocabulary and spelling while having fun.

Hosting a game night

Choose a game everyone can learn in ten minutes, keep rules short, and agree on house rules before starting. Rotate who picks the game so that everyone feels included. Snacks and good lighting help more than you might expect.

Healthy play habits

Games are fun, and a few simple habits keep them that way. These are general suggestions, not medical rules.

  • Take breaks: stand up, stretch and look at something far away every 30 to 60 minutes.
  • Sit well: keep your back supported, feet flat and the screen at roughly eye level.
  • Protect sleep: stop playing at least an hour before bed so your mind can settle.
  • Stay active: balance screen time with movement, outdoor time and friends.
  • Drink water: keep a bottle nearby instead of relying on sugary drinks.
Good to know: if play starts to interfere with school, work, sleep or relationships, or you feel unable to stop, talk to someone you trust or a qualified health professional.

Online safety and a parent guide

Many games let players chat, trade items and spend money. Families can reduce risk with a few clear steps.

For parents and guardians

  1. Use the built-in parental controls on consoles, phones and stores to set age limits, spending limits and play-time limits.
  2. Play together at first so you understand what your child is doing.
  3. Turn off or restrict chat with strangers for younger children.
  4. Explain that real people are behind usernames, and that personal details should never be shared.
  5. Keep a regular conversation open so your child feels safe telling you about anything uncomfortable.

For all players

  • Use a strong, unique password and enable two-step verification.
  • Download games only from official stores to avoid fake or harmful copies.
  • Be careful with links and "free gift" offers, which are often scams.
  • Report and block players who are abusive.

Frequently asked questions

How much screen time is right for children?

It depends on age and the family's needs. Health organisations suggest limiting recreational screen time for young children and keeping a balance with sleep, school and physical activity. Ask your paediatrician for advice about your child.

Do I need an expensive device to enjoy games?

No. Many excellent puzzle, strategy and indie games run on ordinary phones and computers, and tabletop games need no electronics at all.

Are in-app purchases necessary?

Usually not. Many games are fully playable without spending. Set a spending limit or require a password for purchases to avoid surprises.
